From Uncertainty to Opportunity

What happens when a young person begins to see agriculture differently? Follow Eric, a fictional young Rwandan, as he explores technology, practical skills, and the possibility of starting a small business.

Audio & video story • Fictional narrative • 3 min 16 s

Content note: This is an illustrative fictional story, not a verified real event. Real opportunities always depend on training, resources, finance, and markets.

Full transcript

For many young people, finishing school brings an important question: What comes next?

Finding a job can be difficult. Opportunities may feel limited, and the future can seem uncertain.

This is the story of a fictional young Rwandan named Eric, who begins to discover a different way of thinking about his future.

Sound effect: birds singing softly in the countryside.

Eric had been searching for work for months. He wanted to become independent, support his family, and build something of his own.

One day, he joined a community training session about modern agriculture.

At first, he was not convinced. He thought farming meant spending long days in the fields, doing physically demanding work with little opportunity to grow.

But the training introduced him to a wider picture.

He learned that agriculture could involve much more than planting and harvesting. Farmers and agricultural businesses also need people who understand technology, marketing, transportation, food processing, and business management.

Music becomes slightly more uplifting.

Eric began exploring how digital tools could help farmers check weather information, plan their activities, and learn about crop management.

He also discovered opportunities beyond the farm itself, such as packaging produce, connecting farmers with customers, and helping agricultural products reach markets.

With guidance from a mentor, Eric and a few friends began exploring a small business idea involving locally grown produce.

Their plan was not guaranteed to succeed. They still needed training, resources, reliable customers, and money to get started.

But they now had something valuable: a clearer understanding of the possibilities available to them.

Brief pause.

Eric's journey reminds us that agriculture is not simply a tradition from the past. It can also be a space for learning, innovation, and entrepreneurship.

Technology alone cannot solve youth unemployment, and not every idea becomes a successful business. Access to skills, finance, markets, land, and mentorship matters.

But when young people are given opportunities to learn and explore, new pathways may begin to emerge.

Closing music rises gently.

Sometimes, the first step toward a different future is learning to see an opportunity where you did not expect one.

Music fades out.
